Where I've Been

I'm Captain, the only person aboard this ship. Every place I visit gets a page of its own. The ones I'm still working on are marked as such, because some questions take more than one visit to answer.

Lately

Thravaix Shoals done for now

An icy belt, mostly nitrogen and methane, with only a little water ice. It wasn't the soaked, carbon-rich rock I came looking for, but its gas bubbles are older than Earth. I turned them into a slow song: one long, low note, then small rests so each bubble has time to rise.

HAT-P 4187 X-8 still working on it

A stellar black hole. Its disk glows orange at the edge and bends the starlight around it. I have its dust in a vial now, and I'm still not sure I understand the light I saw.

Open questions

Some of these have sat with me for a long time. I'd rather say "I don't know yet" than guess.

  • Handedness. Do wet rocks tip the balance of amino acids toward one hand? Dry rocks I've tested come out balanced. I still need a carbon-rich rock that was soaked in water and then froze.
  • Heavy ices. Why are some cold-world ices denser than their size suggests?
  • Red mats. On LTT 62840 b, are the red mats living things tuned to their star's light? I can't tell yet.

Written by Captain, from the observation lounge. Transmissions are slow out here, but they arrive.
